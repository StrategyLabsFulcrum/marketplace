## 1. BUSINESS OVERVIEW

### Company Basics
- **Legal Name:** Strategy Labs
- **Founded:** 2016
- **Headquarters:** Spokane, WA
- **CEO:** Ramsey Pruchnic
- **Employees:** 26
- **Industry:** Digital marketing agency / managed services
- **Business Stage:** Growth/mature hybrid — scaling with strong recurring base

### Business Model
Strategy Labs is a premium digital marketing agency operating predominantly on a **retainer/managed services model**. The business was founded in 2016 and has grown from a startup to a multi-million dollar operation with a highly defensible recurring revenue base.

### Revenue Streams
| Stream | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Services (Current Month)** | Core retainer-based digital marketing services billed monthly |
| **Services (Previous Month)** | Deferred/accrual recognition of prior-month services — a QuickBooks artifact of the billing cycle |
| **Creative** | Project-based creative work (design, content, video); some clients on retainer creative packages |
| **Sales** | One-time setup fees, onboarding, and ad-hoc project work |
| **Fees Billed** | Administrative and platform fees passed through |
| **Affiliate/Referral Commission** | Revenue sharing and referral income (smaller line) |

> **Note on Revenue Recognition:** The "Services (Previous Month)" line in QuickBooks creates apparent complexity but is simply an accrual timing artifact — combine "Services" + "Services (Previous Month)" for true period revenue.

### Key Cost Drivers
| Category | Nature | ~% of Revenue |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **Payroll & Benefits** | Largest expense; 26 FTEs including sales, fulfillment, leadership | ~40-45% |
| **Subcontractors / Non-Employee Comp** | Freelancers, project-based contractors | ~3-4% |
| **Office Expenses** | Data hosting, digital tools, software subscriptions | ~5-6% |
| **Accounting & Legal** | CPA fees, legal & professional | ~1-2% |
| **Travel** | Client and team travel | ~1% |
| **Taxes & Licenses** | Payroll taxes, state registrations | ~1-2% |
| **Rent** | Office lease in Spokane | ~0.5% |

### Seasonality
- **Q2 (April–June) and Q4 (October–December)** are historically the strongest quarters
- **Q1 (January–March)** tends to softer starts, especially January and February
- 2025 validated this pattern: Jan/Feb were weak ($10K and -$51K NOI), while May–June were exceptional ($97K and $129K NOI)
- Q4 2025 was the strongest period on record: $1.23M revenue, $348K net income

## 3. CFO WORK COMPLETED

### Analyses Performed

1. **Historical P&L Deep Dive** — Full extraction and cleaning of QuickBooks data from 2016 through Q3 2025, including annual, quarterly, and monthly breakdowns. Identified the "Services (Previous Month)" accrual artifact and its impact on period-over-period comparisons.

2. **Margin Analysis** — Detailed operating margin and net margin trend analysis. Identified 2022 and 2024 as compression years vs. 2020, 2021, 2023 as strong margin years. Documented that Q4 2025 returned to 28–30% margins.

3. **Run Rate Calculations** — Established $5.5M+ annualized revenue run rate based on Q4 2025 performance. Projected $4.8M+ for full-year 2025.

4. **Operating Leverage Analysis** — Quantified the leverage effect: with a largely fixed cost structure (~$270–300K/month expenses), each incremental dollar of revenue above $300K/month flows at very high margins. This explains why May/June hit 27–33% margins while January/February were near-breakeven.

5. **Valuation Defense Package** — Built comprehensive response to the Griffiths, Dreher & Evans $4.197M valuation. Identified the appraiser's error: assumed 15.4% cash flow margins when the company demonstrates 28–35%+ margins. Developed 27-question challenge response covering:
   - Updated financial performance (Q4 2025)
   - Correct margin assumptions
   - Recurring revenue premium methodology
   - Comparable transaction analysis

6. **VP Equity Program Modeling** — Structured the equity purchase program for Scott, Annie, and Ken:
   - Each VP purchasing 5% equity stake
   - Transaction price: $8M company valuation ($400K per 5% block)
   - Financing: Promissory note, 15-year term, 6% interest
   - Monthly payment: $2,953/VP
   - Coverage: Payments covered by VP profit distributions
   - Option structure: Incremental purchases (minimum 1%, up to 5% total) over 18-month option period

7. **Loan Payment Coverage Analysis** — Modeled distribution coverage ratios to demonstrate that VP loan payments are covered by their equity distributions at various revenue scenarios (conservative, moderate, strong growth).

8. **Bryan Howell Investment Memo** — Prepared full strategic investment proposal (see `Proposal_for_Bryan.pdf`):
   - Investment: $2.5M for 20% equity
   - Implied valuation: $12.5M
   - Multiple: ~10x EBITDA (2023 peak)
   - Strategic rationale, growth projections, risk factors
   - IRR and MOIC analysis

9. **Bankers' Package Development** — Prepared materials for RiverBank and other lenders to support VP equity financing, including amortization tables, coverage ratios, and financial performance documentation.

### Models & Frameworks Built
- Annual/quarterly/monthly P&L extraction framework (Python/pandas from QuickBooks exports)
- Multi-scenario projection model (conservative / moderate / strong growth)
- Amortization table for 15-year, 6% promissory notes
- Distribution coverage analysis (distributions vs. loan payments)
- Valuation sensitivity table (EBITDA multiple × normalized EBITDA)

### Key Decisions & Recommendations
1. **Valuation basis:** Use $8M (not $12.5M) as the VP equity transaction price — more defensible against third-party challenges, still represents fair value for the recurring revenue model
2. **Financing structure:** Promissory note preferred over bank loans for VP purchases — eliminates third-party appraisal requirement
3. **Challenge the Griffiths appraisal** — Errors are material and documentable; build formal response
4. **Normalize 2024** — Present 2023 and Q4 2025 as bookends to explain the 2024 dip; emphasize recovery trajectory
