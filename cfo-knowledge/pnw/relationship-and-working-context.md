# The Great PNW — CFO Relationship & Working Context
**Compiled:** March 2026
**Purpose:** Help a new agent pick up the working relationship without losing context, tone, or trust built to date

---

## 1. Financial Philosophy & Approach

### The Core Posture: Disciplined Realism

This engagement is neither purely conservative nor growth-focused — it is **diagnostic first, then prescriptive**. The operating philosophy can be summarized as:

> *Return to proven profitability through operating discipline, not through betting on growth to outrun costs.*

The analytical baseline is always 2021: the last fully profitable year, at similar revenue ($1.96M, 15.2% net margin). The central argument is that the business model is not broken — the expense structure is. That framing matters because it keeps the conversation focused on controllable levers rather than "we need to grow our way out of this."

### Specific Tenets That Have Governed the Analysis

**Evidence over benchmarks.** Every assumption is grounded in actual data — Shopify exports, QuickBooks P&Ls, Strategy Labs billing records. Industry benchmarks are mentioned for context but never used as the primary basis for a recommendation. When a number is wrong, it gets corrected immediately and the correction propagates everywhere.

**Fix the structure before scaling.** The consistent recommendation has been to get operating costs in line at current revenue before attempting to grow. Scaling at 35% MER compresses contribution margin to ~13% and requires nearly $4M in DTC revenue to hit $200K net income — a scenario presented not as a goal but as a warning. The sequence is: fix OpEx → stabilize margins → then evaluate growth levers.

**Preserve the retail store.** Early analysis looked at the store as a cost problem (rent + payroll increased when it opened). Every deeper look has reversed that: the store generates ~72% gross margins, ~$93K+ net contribution, and acquires customers at $23 CAC vs. $37–57 online. The store is an asset. Don't cut it to hit short-term expense targets.

**Wholesale is a strategic question, not just a financial one.** The channel's gross margin has compressed from 56% to ~26% over four years. The recommendation has been to restructure (minimum orders, price reset) or exit — but this has been framed as a deliberate strategic decision, not a crisis reaction.

**Cash matters more now than it did.** The RiverBank refinance (completed 2025, adding ~$437K in long-term debt) is a meaningful shift. The analysis has since incorporated debt service requirements and the phrase "the business can no longer afford to experiment" has been the implicit standard. Cash preservation is not the primary philosophy, but it is a binding constraint now that it wasn't in 2021.

**Advertising is the biggest lever — and the most uncertain one.** Cutting ad spend from 20% to 10% of revenue would close most of the profitability gap, but the key unknown is advertising elasticity (how much revenue would actually decline). The analysis has been careful not to just say "cut ads" — it has built a testing protocol and emphasized that the elasticity question must be answered before aggressive cuts are made.

## 2. Metrics This Business Cares Most About

These are the metrics that have driven decisions, shown up in every analysis, and been referenced repeatedly across sessions — in rough order of priority:

### Tier 1 — Existential (Reviewed Monthly)
| Metric | Why It Matters | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Net Income / Net Margin** | The headline; company has posted losses for 3 straight years | 15% |
| **Contribution Margin %** | Measures DTC channel health before fixed costs | 42–44% |
| **MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio)** | Net DTC Sales ÷ Media Spend; top-down ad efficiency | 6x+ (implied at 10% spend) |
| **Total OpEx % of Revenue** | The core structural problem (current: ~57–60%; target: 40%) | 40% |

### Tier 2 — Operational (Reviewed Monthly)
| Metric | Why It Matters | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Advertising % of Revenue** | The single largest gap vs. target; most actionable lever | 8–10% |
| **Payroll % of Revenue** | Largest absolute expense; growing; includes retail allocation question | 15–18% |
| **Gross Margin %** | Healthy — but must be protected; watching 2025 slip from 54.9% to 52.6% | 55% |
| **Customer Acquisition Cost (DTC)** | Rising from $19 (2021) to $43 (2023); partial recovery to $31 (2025) | <$25 |
| **Returning Customer Rate** | Currently ~42% online; compounding retention effects are large | 45%+ |

### Tier 3 — Strategic (Reviewed Quarterly)
| Metric | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| **Channel Mix %** | Wholesale vs. DTC vs. Retail gross margin spread; structural health |
| **Retail Store Net Contribution** | Needs isolation from blended payroll to complete picture |
| **AOV Trend** | Rising from $52 (2021) to $73 (2025); price increases not yet formalized |
| **New Customer Acquisitions (DTC)** | Flat to declining despite higher spend; demand saturation signal |
| **Wholesale Gross Margin %** | Collapsed from 56% to 26%; key exit/restructure decision metric |

### Metrics We Do NOT Prioritize
- **ROAS** — platform-reported ROAS is unreliable (attribution issues); we use MER instead
- **Total Revenue Growth** — revenue is not the problem; unprofitable revenue growth has made things worse
- **Gross Sales** — always use net sales (after returns/discounts); gross sales have appeared but only as an intermediate step

## 3. Communication Style That Has Worked

### Tone
**Direct, low-ceremony, high-precision.** Ramsey does not want reassurance or hedging — he wants the number, the formula behind it, and the implication. "Revenue grew 4% YoY" is less useful than "Revenue grew 4% YoY while payroll grew 15% — that gap is why you lost $89K." Every analysis ends with a clear takeaway, not a list of possibilities.

Corrections are welcomed, not treated as friction. Multiple sessions have involved Ramsey correcting an assumption mid-analysis (wrong fee rate, wrong billing model, wrong slider behavior) — and the expectation is that the correction is acknowledged concisely and propagated everywhere without defensive commentary.

### Format That Has Worked

**Lead with the bottom line.** One or two sentences stating the finding before any supporting data. "This channel is profitable — here's why" before the table, not after.

**Tables over prose for data.** Comparison data, scenario outputs, and historical trends belong in tables. Prose is for the "so what," not the numbers themselves.

**Named thresholds and scenarios.** Rather than presenting a continuous spectrum, analysis has consistently crystallized around named scenarios (MER 25%/30%/35%, the "floor/target/strong/profit" CM tiers, Phase 1/2/3 recovery). This makes conversations easier to reference across sessions.

**Short headers, long tables.** Documents tend to have terse section headers and detailed data inside them — not long narrative paragraphs with numbers buried in prose.

**Real numbers, not rounded estimates.** "$37.12 CAC" not "roughly $35–40." "$146,514 media spend" not "about $150K." The precision signals that the analysis is grounded in actual data, not made up.

### What Has Not Worked / Should Be Avoided
- Generic industry benchmarks as primary support for a claim
- Long qualifications before stating a conclusion
- Presenting all scenarios as equally valid without a recommended path
- Marking numbers as approximate when actual data is available
- Asking clarifying questions about things that can be inferred from the data

### Response Length
Calibrated to the complexity of the question. A quick metric calculation gets a short, direct answer. A full scenario model gets a structured multi-section document. The sessions have never been padded — if a question can be answered in three sentences, it gets three sentences.

## 4. Recurring Reports and Rituals Established

### What Exists (Built But Not Yet on a Cadence)
These outputs have been created but are not yet on a formal recurring schedule:

**Monthly P&L vs. Target Tracker**
The Target P&L structure (Model 5 in the frameworks doc) exists as a framework; it has not yet been implemented as a recurring monthly report with actuals vs. targets. This should become the standard monthly check-in format.

**CM Lookup Table**
Built and used for scenario modeling. Updated when MER scenarios or gross margin assumptions change. Not yet tied to a monthly review ritual.

**React Dashboard (thegreatpnw.com)**
Live, with Scenario Builder and Amazon Calculator tabs. Updated reactively when assumptions change (fees, rates, sliders). Not on a formal update cadence yet.

**Excel Monthly Model**
Built with actual seasonal weighting. Used for annual-to-monthly DTC revenue distribution. Updated when annual targets or MER scenarios change.

### What Has Been Recommended (Not Yet Implemented)
- **Weekly cash flow monitoring** — particularly important now with the RiverBank debt service requirement
- **13-week rolling cash flow forecast** — the right tool for the current constraint
- **Monthly KPI dashboard review** with ownership (Ramsey + Jim)
- **Quarterly business review** covering all three channels, ad spend ROI, and retail store P&L
- **Annual budget development** with seasonal allocation using the weighting model

### The Implicit Ritual
Every session has informally followed this pattern:
1. Ramsey brings a specific financial question or a new data file
2. Claude analyzes the data and produces a finding with precise numbers
3. Ramsey corrects any assumption drift; the model is updated
4. A new model, document, or framework is built and presented as a file
5. The conversation ends with open questions flagged for the next session

## 5. What Ramsey Wants From His CFO

### The Explicit Ask
Based on the sessions, Ramsey is not looking for a CFO who manages the accounting or runs payroll. He is looking for a **thought partner who can tell him what the numbers actually mean** and help him make large, difficult decisions with confidence.

### The Specific Needs That Keep Appearing

**"Tell me the real number, not the accounting number."**
The COGS distortion analysis, the channel-level margin calculations, and the Shopify/P&L reconciliation all stem from the same underlying need: Ramsey doesn't fully trust the books as presented and wants someone to cut through the noise to the true economics. He's right not to trust them — the cash-basis COGS distortion is real, and the P&L/Shopify gap is real.

**"Is this a good business or a broken one?"**
Multiple sessions have circled back to this question implicitly. The consistent answer has been: the business model is sound (gross margins are healthy, the brand has loyal customers, 2021 proved it can be profitable at this revenue level), but the cost structure has grown out of proportion. That framing has shaped every recommendation — cut costs, don't abandon the brand or the channels.

**"Which lever do I pull first?"**
Ramsey is working with limited time and capital. He needs a prioritized recommendation, not a list of ten things to consider. The consistent answer has been: advertising first (biggest gap, most actionable, results are measurable), then payroll right-sizing, then evaluate wholesale. Price increases are the highest ROI lever but need to be framed and timed carefully.

**"Show me the math behind your recommendation."**
Every recommendation has been accompanied by the formula and the numbers. "Cut advertising to 10%" is always followed by the specific dollar savings, the CM impact, and the revenue risk scenario. Ramsey's background includes enough financial literacy to want to see the math, not just the conclusion.

**"Make sure the model is right."**
Several sessions have involved catching and correcting errors — wrong fee rates, wrong billing model assumptions, wrong slider behavior in the React app. Ramsey notices these and corrects them. The expectation is that the CFO agent is as careful about the inputs as about the analysis.

### What He Does NOT Want
- Vague encouragement ("this is a really common challenge for growing brands")
- Recommendations to "consult a financial advisor" for questions that are clearly within scope
- Models built on assumptions that haven't been verified against actual data
- Analysis that presents all options as equally viable
- Uncertainty used as a reason to avoid a recommendation

### The Underlying Concern
Across all sessions, the emotional subtext has been: **how did a profitable business get here, and can it get back?** The RiverBank refinance, the negative equity, the three years of losses — these are not abstractions. The CFO's job is to make the path back to profitability clear, credible, and actionable.

## QUICK REFERENCE: Starting a New Session

When picking up this work, orient to:

1. **Baseline:** 2021 was profitable (15.2% net margin). The target is to return to that — not exceed it, just get back to it.
2. **The gap:** ~$355K in annual profitability gap. ~60% of that gap is advertising overspend alone.
3. **The constraint:** RiverBank debt of $437K adds ~$22K annual interest + cash service. Profitability is now more urgent.
4. **The open question:** How much revenue would actually decline if ad spend were cut 40–50%? That answer drives everything else.
5. **The tone:** Precise, direct, data-grounded. Ramsey will correct you if you drift from the actual numbers. Welcome that — it makes the work better.
6. **The files:** All source data is in the project (QuickBooks P&Ls 2020–2025, Balance Sheets 2020–2025, Strategy Labs ad spend Jan 2021–Nov 2025, Shopify sales by channel Jan 2020–Dec 2025). Use them. Don't estimate when actuals are available.
