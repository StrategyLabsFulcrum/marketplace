### Business Model Details

**Pricing Architecture (Keystone):**
- Manufacturing cost = 1x
- Wholesale price = 2x manufacturing cost (50% gross margin before commission)
- DTC/Retail price = 4x manufacturing cost (target ~75% gross margin)

**Wholesale margin compression:**
- Wholesale gross margin before commission: ~50%
- Sales rep commission: 10% of wholesale price
- Effective wholesale margin: ~40% of wholesale revenue
- Licensed products (Smokey Bear, Rainier Beer) add 10% royalty on top → margin ~30%

**Licensing agreements:**
- **Smokey Bear** (U.S. Forest Service) — 10% royalty; key brand differentiator; drives traffic
- **Rainier Beer** — 10% royalty; regional relevance
- Royalties recorded in Cost of Sales (2024: $16,566; 2025: $16,001)

**Key Product Categories:** Tees, Hoodies, Crewnecks, Flannels, Fleece, Outerwear, Hats/Beanies, Accessories (Bags, Socks, Belts, Drinkware, Stickers)

**Key Collections:** Smokey Bear (licensed), Upper Left USA™, Woodsy, Miller Collection (core best-seller), Essentials

**Membership Program:** Upper Left Club — monthly subscription delivering limited edition tees. Recurring revenue opportunity that remains underdeveloped.

### Manufacturing & Operations
- The company designs, manufactures, warehouses, and ships products in-house from Spokane
- Primary printing partner: **Bison Printing** ($157,761 in 2024; $123,623 in 2025)
- Warehousing and fulfillment handled internally
- Office/warehouse located at 2808 N Monroe, Spokane

### Seasonality
- **Q1 (Jan–Mar): Consistently weakest quarter** — deep operating losses every year
  - Q1 2023 net income: -$128,052
  - Q1 2024 net income: -$108,796
- **Q4 (Oct–Dec): Strongest quarter** — holiday peak drives profitability
  - Q4 2022 net income: $166,316 (25.2% margin)
- **Q2–Q3:** Variable; summer outdoor season creates moderate demand spike
- Ad spend tracks seasonality: low in Q1, peaks July, ramps for holiday

### Key Personnel / Stakeholders
- **Ramsey** — owner/operator; primary financial decision-maker; also handles technical development (React, Excel modeling)
- **Jim** — collaborates on financial analysis and expense baseline work
- **Strategy Labs (SL)** — paid media agency managing DTC advertising

  - Billing structure Amazon: **10% of gross Amazon sales**
  - *These two billing models are distinct and must never be conflated*
- **Joel** — co-owner (based on equity accounts in balance sheets; large negative distributions)

## 3. CFO WORK COMPLETED

### Analyses Completed

**1. P&L Trend Analysis (2020–2025)**
- Full year-over-year comparison across all expense categories
- Identified operating expenses as the core problem (not gross margin, which is healthy)
- Isolated that the 2023–2024 deterioration was driven by advertising overspend + payroll growth

**2. Channel Profitability Framework**
- Analyzed DTC vs. Wholesale vs. Retail store contribution
- Established that gross margin is structurally sound; problem is OpEx allocation
- Identified wholesale as structurally problematic: low margin after commission + royalties

**3. Retail Store Profitability Assessment**
- Revenue: $464K (2024), $376K (2025)
- Direct costs: ~$42K store rent + retail staff wages
- Established: Retail store is actually more valuable than it appears — generates strong gross margins, meaningful net contribution, and serves as a low-CAC customer acquisition channel
- Key insight: Store CAC is significantly cheaper than online acquisition

**4. Advertising Efficiency Analysis**
- Built advertising spend timeline from Strategy Labs data (Jan 2021 – Nov 2025, monthly)
- Calculated MER by year; identified MER decline from ~9x to ~5x as structural stress signal
- Developed advertising elasticity testing protocol to determine how much ad spend can be safely reduced
- Q1 2025 ad spend significantly reduced (~$30K vs. $54K in Q1 2024) — implementing cost discipline

**5. Seasonal Cost Analysis**
- Identified Q1 as structurally loss-making due to fixed costs vs. low seasonal revenue
- Q1 consistently generates large losses: -$108K (2024), -$128K (2023)
- Recommendation: Variable cost structure for slow season; reduce retail hours in Q1

**6. MER Scenario Modeling**
- Built contribution margin lookup table across MER scenarios (25%, 30%, 35%)
- Shows required net sales, gross sales, media spend, agency fees, and total marketing cost for monthly CM targets
- CM definition: Net DTC Sales − COGS − Media Spend − Agency Fees
- Baseline 2025 CM: ~40%; target: 42–44%

**7. Wholesale Channel Evaluation**
- True margin after commission: ~40% of wholesale revenue
- Licensed product wholesale margin: ~30%
- Evaluation: Channel is barely profitable; high revenue volatility (ranged from $12K in 2022 to $579K in 2023)
- Recommendation: Evaluate exit or restructure (raise minimums, increase prices)

**8. New vs. Returning Customer Economics**
- Returning customers cost a fraction of new customers to reactivate
- Modeled compounding retention effects — modest improvement sustained over years generates substantial cumulative revenue
- Conclusion: Retention investment is highly efficient vs. acquisition spend

**9. Balance Sheet Analysis (2020–2025)**
- Tracked equity deterioration from +$341K (2021) to -$84K (2025)
- Identified and flagged the RiverBank debt restructuring as meaningful P&L burden going forward
- Mapped long-term liability changes: FIB loans paid off, replaced by RiverBank term loan + LOC

**10. E-Commerce Funnel Assessment**
- Sessions: 574,548 (2025 partial data)
- Add-to-cart rate: 5.53% (above industry average)
- Cart-to-purchase: 32.06% (solid)
- Checkout completion: 49.5% — identified as primary opportunity (50% abandonment)
- YoY: +64% add-to-carts, +43% checkouts, +21% completions

**11. COGS Cash vs. Accrual Distortion Analysis**
- Identified that accountant records inventory purchases as COGS when purchased (cash basis), not when sold
- Results in months with large inventory buys appearing artificially unprofitable
- Completed analysis and correction guide — implementation pending

### Models/Frameworks Built
- MER scenario model (25%, 30%, 35%) with monthly CM lookup table
- Excel-based monthly financial models using actual seasonal weighting from SL ad spend data
- React web dashboard at thegreatpnw.com with multiple tabs:
  - Amazon Calculator
  - Scenario Builder
  - (Ongoing development)
- Advertising elasticity testing protocol
- Compounding retention effects model

### Key Strategic Decisions/Recommendations Made
1. **Cut advertising to 10% of revenue** — current 18–20% is primary profitability driver
2. **Right-size payroll to 16% of revenue** — current 20–22% is unsustainable
3. **Preserve retail store** — net contributor despite higher fixed costs; low CAC channel
4. **Evaluate wholesale exit or restructure** — structurally thin margins + volatility
5. **Price increase lever** — identified as path to hit CM targets without requiring MER compression
6. **Fix checkout abandonment** — 50% dropout rate; biggest single DTC lever
7. **Implement seasonal cost variability** — Q1 must reduce costs to match revenue
8. **Investigate revenue discrepancy** — gap between P&L and Shopify data; "Draft Orders" likely = manually entered wholesale orders

## 6. CFO ROLE DEFINITION FOR THIS BRAND

### What "Acting as CFO" Means Here
The fractional CFO role for The Great PNW is not traditional corporate finance. It is:
- **Diagnostic and strategic** — identifying root causes of loss and building the path back to profitability
- **Hands-on modeling** — building actual Excel and React financial tools, not just providing commentary
- **Channel-level** — thinking in DTC, Wholesale, and Retail as distinct profit centers
- **Data-grounded** — all analysis must tie to actual Shopify, QBO, or SL data, not industry benchmarks
- **Decision-support focused** — Ramsey makes calls; CFO provides the financial clarity to make good ones

### Decisions the CFO Needs to Support
- Should we reduce ad spend, and by how much, without destroying revenue?
- Should we exit or restructure wholesale?
- Is the retail store earning its keep?
- Can we afford a price increase, and what does it do to margin?
- How do we manage cash given the RiverBank debt load?
- Which expense categories need to be cut first and by how much?
- What does a path to 15% net margin actually look like, quarter by quarter?

### Stakeholders and Their Needs
- **Ramsey (owner/operator):** Needs decision-ready financial analysis; not interested in vague guidance; prefers precise, model-driven answers grounded in actual data
- **Jim:** Financial analysis collaborator; works on expense baseline and cost analysis
- **Strategy Labs:** Receives ad budget guidance; bills at 20% of media spend (DTC) and 10% of gross Amazon sales — these are separate billing models
- **Accountant:** Needs guidance on COGS accrual correction; currently on cash basis

### Work Cadence
- **Monthly:** P&L review, expense vs. target tracking, channel performance check
- **Quarterly:** Full business review; ad spend ROI; retail store profitability check
- **Ad hoc:** Scenario modeling when decisions arise (price increases, channel exits, new hires)
- **Annual:** Budget development, strategic planning

### CFO Behavioral Standards (Based on Ramsey's Preferences)
- Always ground analysis in actual numbers, not estimates
- State key metrics precisely — use defined formulas (e.g., CM = Net DTC Sales − COGS − Media − Agency Fees; MER = Net DTC Revenue ÷ Media Spend)
- When model assumptions drift, correct them immediately and propagate changes through all affected models
- Never conflate DTC agency fees (20% of media spend) with Amazon agency fees (10% of gross sales)
- Lead with the bottom line, then support with data
- Flag when a question requires data not yet available

### Important Business Context
- **The retail store is more valuable than it appears on the surface.** Despite adding ~$42K rent + retail staff wages, it generates strong gross margins, meaningful net contribution, and functions as a low-CAC customer acquisition channel cheaper than online acquisition.
- **Wholesale is structurally problematic.** Margins have compressed dramatically. Revenue is volatile (ranged from $12K to $579K across four years). Exiting or restructuring is under active evaluation.
- **Advertising is the #1 profitability lever.** Bringing ad spend from 18–20% to 8–10% of revenue would close most of the profitability gap. The open question is whether revenue would hold.
- **Payroll grew partly from intentional retail investment.** The 2023 store opening added real structural cost. The issue is whether remaining payroll is right-sized for current revenue.
- **The accrual vs. cash COGS problem is material.** Months with large inventory purchases appear artificially unprofitable. This distorts channel and period analysis until corrected.
- **New vs. returning customer economics are dramatically different.** Returning customers reactivate at a fraction of new customer CAC. Retention investment is highly efficient.
- **The company has been profitable before at similar revenue.** 2021: $1.96M revenue, 15.2% net margin. This proves the business model works — the problem is operating expense creep, not a broken product or market.
- **Billing model precision is non-negotiable.** DTC: SL bills 20% of media spend. Amazon: SL bills 10% of gross Amazon sales. These are separate structures and must stay separate in all models, reports, and UI.
- **The RiverBank refinance is the critical new variable for 2026.** ~$437K in new long-term debt with a term loan + LOC adds meaningful cash service requirements. Profitability is now more urgent, not less.
