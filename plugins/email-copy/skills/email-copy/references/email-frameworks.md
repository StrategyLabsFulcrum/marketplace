# Email Copy Frameworks

Sequence structures, subject line formulas, and email-type-specific guidance.

---

## Sequence Arc Structures

### Welcome Series (5 emails)

The welcome series is the highest-leverage email sequence. New subscribers have peak interest and open rates. Squander it with generic content and you lose them.

| Email | Timing | Purpose | Copy Focus |
|-------|--------|---------|-----------|
| 1 — Welcome | Immediate | Set expectations; deliver on the promise | Warmth, specificity, what's coming next |
| 2 — Brand story | Day 2 | Build connection; why this brand exists | Origin, belief, "you're in the right place" |
| 3 — Best content/value | Day 4 | Demonstrate expertise; build trust | Highest-value insight or content; no sell |
| 4 — Social proof | Day 6 | Build conviction; show results | Customer story or transformation; soft offer |
| 5 — First offer | Day 8 | Convert; make the case clearly | Benefit-first offer with risk reversal |

**Welcome series tone:** Warmer and more personal than later emails. The reader just raised their hand — reciprocate with genuine value before asking for anything.

### Promotional Campaign (3–5 emails)

Announces and drives urgency around a specific offer.

| Email | Timing | Purpose | Copy Focus |
|-------|--------|---------|-----------|
| 1 — Announcement | Day 0 | Introduce the offer | Benefit-led; what they get; why now |
| 2 — Benefit deep-dive | Day 2 | Build desire; address hesitation | Feature/benefit expansion; social proof |
| 3 — Urgency escalation | Day 4 | Create action urgency | Deadline reminder; stakes of missing out |
| 4 — Last day | Final day | Final push | "Last chance" — direct, no fluff |
| 5 — Extended (optional) | Day after | Capture missed conversions | Brief extension with a reason |

**Promotional tone:** More direct than the welcome series. The reader knows what you're offering — get there faster. Each email should be shorter than the one before.

### Abandoned Cart (3 emails)

The highest-ROI automated sequence in e-commerce. These readers were close — help them over the line.

| Email | Timing | Purpose | Copy Focus |
|-------|--------|---------|-----------|
| 1 — Reminder | 1 hour | Gentle reminder; they may have been interrupted | Friendly, not pushy; show the cart; easy return |
| 2 — Benefit reinforcement | 24 hours | Why this product? Why now? | Product benefits; address common objection; social proof |
| 3 — Incentive or urgency | 48–72 hours | Last push; optional offer | Discount or "selling fast" or both; clear CTA |

**Abandoned cart tone:** Understanding, not accusatory. "You left something behind" not "You forgot to buy." Assume positive intent — life interrupted them.

### Nurture Sequence (5–8 emails)

Educational sequence for leads who aren't ready to buy. Build relationship and expertise; let conversion happen naturally.

| Email | Purpose | Copy Focus |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Establish expertise | Best insight on their primary problem |
| 2 | Deepen problem awareness | What's really causing the problem (reframe) |
| 3 | Introduce solution category | There's a better way; introduce the approach |
| 4 | Social proof | Customer story that mirrors their situation |
| 5 | Objection handling | Address the most common reason they haven't acted |
| 6 | Case study | Detailed result story — before/after |
| 7 | Soft offer | Here's how to work with us; no pressure |
| 8 | Direct offer | Time-limited or clear CTA |

**Nurture tone:** Generous, educational, peer-to-peer. The brand is a trusted advisor, not a salesperson. Sell the problem, not the product, for the first half of the sequence.

### Re-Engagement (3–4 emails)

For subscribers who have stopped opening. Goal: re-engage or get permission to remove them (protecting deliverability).

| Email | Purpose | Copy Focus |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Pattern interrupt | Unexpected subject line; acknowledge the silence; offer value |
| 2 | "We noticed" | Personal tone; ask if they still want to hear from you |
| 3 | Stake the relationship | "We're going to remove you in 7 days unless..." |
| 4 | Goodbye (to non-responders) | Brief, warm; they can always come back; actually remove them |

**Re-engagement tone:** Direct but warm. Do not guilt-trip. The honest approach ("we haven't heard from you in a while — still interested?") performs better than desperate discounting.

### Cold Outreach Sequence (3 emails)

For prospecting email to a cold audience who has not opted in.

| Email | Timing | Purpose | Copy Focus |
|-------|--------|---------|-----------|
| 1 — Introduction | Day 0 | Earn attention; prove relevance | Specific personalization; single insight or value; one ask |
| 2 — Follow-up | Day 5 | Different angle; add value | New reason to respond; briefer than email 1 |
| 3 — Close the loop | Day 10 | Give them an easy out | "This is my last email — still happy to connect if timing changes" |

**Cold outreach rules:**
- Maximum 3 emails. After 3 with no response, they're not interested. Do not email again.
- Always provide an easy one-click opt-out
- Reference their specific situation in email 1 — no merge-field personalization that's obviously automated
- Never send attachments in cold email — spam filters and zero trust

## Subject Line Formulas

### Highest-performing patterns (ranked by average open rate lift):

**1. The curiosity gap**
"What I learned after 6 months of [thing]"
"The one thing [relevant expert] does differently"
"Why [counterintuitive claim]"
"What nobody tells you about [topic]"

**2. Specific numbers**
"3 things I changed (and one ROAS went from 1.2× to 4.8×)"
"[Number] [category] that took our [metric] from [A] to [B]"

**3. Personal and direct**
"[Name], quick question"
"Honestly, I almost didn't send this"
"I was wrong about [thing]"

**4. Direct offer**
"Your [discount/free thing/access] is inside"
"[Name], here's your [X]"
"This expires [day] — [thing]"

**5. Urgency with substance**
"Last day — and here's why it actually matters"
"[Offer] closes at midnight (here's who it's for)"
"48 hours left — [reminder of value]"

**6. The question**
"Have you ever [relatable situation]?"
"Is [problem] killing your [outcome]?"
"What would you do with [desirable outcome]?"

**7. Contrarian/pattern interrupt**
"Stop [thing everyone does]"
"[Popular belief] is wrong"
"This is not a sales email"

### Subject line testing guidance

Always write 3+ subject line options and label one as recommended. A/B testing guidance:
- **Winner definition:** Higher open rate after 20% test sample, minimum 4–6 hours
- **What to test:** Curiosity vs. direct offer; with vs. without emoji; question vs. statement
- **Do not test simultaneously:** Subject line + send time + content (one variable at a time)

### Preview text rules

Preview text (the snippet visible after the subject line in inbox view) should:
- **Extend** the subject hook — not repeat it
- Add a second reason to open, or complete the thought started in the subject
- 45–100 characters for best display across clients

Good: Subject: "Why I stopped sending weekly emails" / Preview: "And the thing that actually worked instead"
Bad: Subject: "Our summer sale is here!" / Preview: "Click to open this email" (auto-generated — always write it manually)

## Email Format Standards

### Body structure guide

**Short email (150–300 words) — promotional, reminder, cart:**
```
[Hook — 1–2 sentences]

[Core message — 3–5 sentences max]

[CTA button]

[PS — optional urgency or second hook]
```

**Medium email (300–500 words) — announcement, benefit email, offer:**
```
[Opening hook]

[Bridge — connect hook to offer/message]

[Core message — benefit or story with specifics]

[Social proof — 1 testimonial or data point]

[CTA]

[PS]
```

**Long email (500–800 words) — story, nurture, case study:**
```
[Opening hook — story or provocative observation]

[Story body — tension, revelation, resolution]

[Bridge to brand/product relevance]

[Proof or outcome]

[CTA — softer; "see how we can help" not "buy now"]

[PS — optional]
```

### Formatting rules

- **Short paragraphs:** 1–3 sentences maximum. White space is your friend in email.
- **Bold key phrases** — not randomly, but for the reader who is scanning, bold should tell the story by itself
- **Bullet points:** Use for lists of 3+ items; never bullet a single item
- **No walls of text:** If you write more than 4 sentences without a line break, break it up
- **Images:** Email copy should work without images (many clients block them). Alt text is mandatory.
- **One CTA:** One primary button per email. A second text link is acceptable in longer emails.

## Compliance Checklist

Every email must comply before it is sent:

**CAN-SPAM (US) requirements:**
- [ ] Clear identification that this is an advertisement (commercial emails)
- [ ] Physical mailing address of the sender in the footer
- [ ] Clear and conspicuous unsubscribe mechanism
- [ ] Unsubscribe honored within 10 business days
- [ ] No deceptive subject lines

**GDPR (EU) requirements:**
- [ ] Email recipient has given explicit opt-in consent (cannot be assumed)
- [ ] Clear identity of the sender
- [ ] Unsubscribe mechanism present
- [ ] Data processing statement available (usually a privacy policy link)

**CASL (Canada) requirements:**
- [ ] Express or implied consent (implied consent has a time limit)
- [ ] Sender identification
- [ ] Unsubscribe mechanism that works within 10 business days

**MAGY Authentication Requirements — enforced, not optional (Gmail, Yahoo, Microsoft Outlook, Apple Mail = 90%+ of inboxes):**
- [ ] SPF record configured for sending domain
- [ ] DKIM record configured (minimum 1024-bit key; 2048-bit recommended)
- [ ] DMARC policy configured (minimum `p=none`; `p=quarantine` or `p=reject` preferred)
- [ ] "From" address uses your own domain — **never @gmail.com or @yahoo.com**
- [ ] Branded sending domain configured (required for lists over 5,000 profiles — removes "sent via klaviyomail.com" and gives you control over sender reputation)
- [ ] One-click unsubscribe present AND requests honored within **2 days** (Gmail/Yahoo requirement — stricter than CAN-SPAM's 10 business days)
- [ ] Spam rate below **0.10%** — monitor via Google Postmaster Tools; hitting 0.30% can result in sending blocked entirely
- [ ] Microsoft enforcement active as of May 2025 — same SPF/DKIM/DMARC requirements apply to Outlook/Hotmail/Live recipients

*Enforcement timeline: Gmail/Yahoo Feb 2024 → Microsoft May 2025 → Gmail increased rejections Nov 2025*

**General deliverability:**
- [ ] Avoid spam trigger words in subject: "FREE", "GUARANTEED", "ACT NOW", "!!!", all caps
- [ ] Plain text version matches HTML version (required for some ISPs)
- [ ] Image-to-text ratio: do not send emails that are primarily one large image with minimal text
- [ ] From name is recognizable and consistent (not a random email address)
