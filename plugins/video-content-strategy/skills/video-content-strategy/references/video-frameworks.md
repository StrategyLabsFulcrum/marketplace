# Video Frameworks Reference

Proven structures for video content across formats and platforms. Use as starting points, not rigid templates.

---

## Hook Formulas by Platform

### TikTok / Instagram Reels (first 1–2 seconds)

The hook must work without sound (many users don't have sound on) AND with sound.

| Hook Type | Example | Best For |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Provocative statement | "This [common advice] is completely wrong." | Contrarian/educational |
| Curiosity gap | "Here's what nobody tells you about [topic]..." | Educational |
| Direct result | "I made $10K in 30 days doing this." | Social proof / aspiration |
| Relatable scenario | "POV: You just [relatable frustration]" | Community / entertainment |
| Visual disruption | [Something unexpected in frame] + caption text | Any |
| Countdown/list | "3 things every [target audience] needs to know" | Educational |
| Question | "Are you still [doing common mistake]?" | Problem-aware audience |

### YouTube (first 30 seconds)

YouTube viewers have chosen to watch — the hook can be slightly longer, but must still establish value quickly.

**Standard YouTube cold open structure:**
1. State the key promise or result (5 sec)
2. Tease what they'll learn or get (5–10 sec)
3. Brief credibility/why you (5–10 sec)
4. Hard cut to content (not into intro animation)

**Advanced hook: "B-roll then promise"**
Open with high-quality B-roll of the result/transformation while VO delivers the hook. Creates visual interest while establishing the value proposition before the camera cuts to the host.

## Complete Video Structures

### The Problem-Aware Tutorial (YouTube, 8–15 min)
Best for: How-to content, educational, search-driven

```
Hook (30 sec): State the specific outcome this video delivers
Context (1 min): Who this is for + why this matters right now
Common mistakes (2–3 min): What most people do wrong + why it fails
The right approach (4–6 min): Step-by-step. The core content.
Advanced tips (1–2 min): 2–3 extras that add value beyond the basics
Recap + next steps (1 min): Summarize + what to do next + CTA
```

### The Thought Leadership Essay (YouTube, 5–12 min)
Best for: Authority building, brand positioning, category education

```
Provocative thesis (30 sec): Your contrarian or non-obvious take
The conventional wisdom (1 min): What most people believe
Why conventional wisdom is wrong (2–3 min): Evidence and reasoning
Your framework / alternative (2–3 min): The better way of thinking
Implications and examples (2 min): What this means in practice
Call to reflection (30 sec): A question to leave them thinking
CTA (30 sec)
```

### The Transformation Story (YouTube, 5–10 min or TikTok/Reels 30–60 sec)
Best for: Social proof, testimonials, product demonstrations

```
Result first: Show the "after" before explaining the "before"
Before state: The problem, struggle, failed attempts
The turning point: What changed (the product, decision, approach)
The process: How it worked — specific, detailed, credible
The result: What life looks like now — tangible, specific
The invitation: "If that sounds like you..."
CTA
```

### The 3-Part Framework (YouTube, 6–12 min or adapted for TikTok)
Best for: Educational content, positioning as an expert

```
Hook: A problem + "most people approach it wrong"
The framework: [Name it] — 3 components, each with:
  - What it is (20 sec)
  - Why it matters (20 sec)
  - How to do it (60–90 sec for YouTube; condensed for short-form)
Common mistakes within each component
Putting it together: How the 3 parts work as a system
CTA
```

## Short-Form Structures (TikTok / Reels, 15–60 sec)

### The "Secret" Format
```
[0–2 sec] "Nobody talks about this [topic] trick"
[2–10 sec] Build the tension: what happens without this knowledge
[10–40 sec] The reveal — clear, specific, actionable
[40–50 sec] "Save this so you don't forget"
```

### The Before/After
```
[0–3 sec] After: Show the result (compelling visual)
[3–15 sec] Before: Show the problem state
[15–40 sec] The transformation — how to get there
[40–50 sec] "Comment X if you want to try this"
```

### The List
```
[0–2 sec] "[Number] things [target audience] needs to know"
[2–40 sec] Each item — rapid fire, 5–8 sec per item
[40–50 sec] CTA or summary
```

### The Reaction / Duet Hook
```
[0–3 sec] React to common advice: "I keep seeing people say [X] and I have to say something"
[3–30 sec] Explain why [X] is wrong and what actually works
[30–50 sec] What to do instead
```

## Video Ad Structures

### Direct Response — 15-Second
```
0–3 sec: Visual hook + problem statement
3–8 sec: Product in action
8–12 sec: Key benefit + result
12–15 sec: CTA + offer
```

### Direct Response — 30-Second
```
0–3 sec: Disruptive hook
3–8 sec: The problem (relatable)
8–18 sec: Product as solution — demonstration
18–25 sec: Social proof (number, quote, or visual)
25–30 sec: CTA + offer + urgency
```

### Direct Response — 60-Second (Full Direct Response)
```
0–5 sec: Hook
5–15 sec: Problem agitation
15–30 sec: Solution intro + product demo
30–45 sec: Proof (testimonial or results)
45–55 sec: Offer + risk reversal
55–60 sec: CTA
```

### Brand Awareness — 6-Second Bumper (YouTube)
```
0–2 sec: Brand/product instantly visible
2–4 sec: One single core message or visual
4–6 sec: Brand mark + tagline
```
*One message only. No complexity.*

### UGC-Style Ad (Performs well on TikTok and Meta)
```
0–3 sec: Creator on camera — "I have to talk about this [product]"
3–15 sec: Authentic problem story (unscripted feel, first person)
15–30 sec: Product in use — genuine demonstration
30–45 sec: Result — specific and personal
45–55 sec: "I got mine from [brand]" + product on screen
55–60 sec: Promo code or CTA
```

## Thumbnail Design Principles (YouTube)

Thumbnails are the primary driver of click-through rate. The video title and thumbnail work together.

**The 4 elements of a high-CTR thumbnail:**
1. **Emotional face** (if applicable) — Human faces with clear emotional expressions drive clicks
2. **The result or transformation** — Show the outcome, not the process
3. **Contrast and clarity** — Should be legible at 100×56px (mobile search list size)
4. **Text (3–5 words max)** — The thumbnail text and video title should complement, not repeat each other

**Thumbnail/title split-test combinations:**
- Title: the hook → Thumbnail: the visual proof
- Title: the question → Thumbnail: the answer teased visually
- Title: the number → Thumbnail: the contrast (before/after)

**Color strategy:**
- Use brand colors consistently but add contrast against YouTube's white background
- Red and yellow have the highest visibility at thumbnail size
- Avoid muddy, low-contrast images

## SEO Frameworks for YouTube

### Title Formula
`[Primary Keyword] — [Compelling Secondary Hook]`

Examples:
- "Email Marketing for Beginners — What Nobody Tells You"
- "How to Run Facebook Ads in 2026 (Updated Strategy)"
- "Instagram Growth: The 3-Part System That Actually Works"

**Title rules:**
- Primary keyword near the front
- Under 60 characters for desktop; key information in first 40 characters for mobile
- No clickbait — YouTube penalizes high-click, low-retention combinations
- Include the year for "how-to" content to signal freshness

### Description Formula (First 150 characters are shown without "more")
```
[Hook — mirrors the hook from the video. Restate the core promise.]

In this video you'll learn:
• [Specific thing 1]
• [Specific thing 2]
• [Specific thing 3]

[Timestamps — if video is 8+ minutes]

[Links mentioned in video]

[Channel CTA: Subscribe for [specific value]]

[Brand links: website, social, offers]

[Keywords: naturally integrated, not stuffed]
```

### Tag Strategy
Use 10–15 tags:
- 2–3: Exact-match primary keyword phrases
- 3–5: Related secondary keywords
- 2–3: Broader category terms
- 2–3: Channel-specific consistent tags (brand name, recurring series name)

## Retention Optimization Techniques

**Pattern interrupts:** Every 60–90 seconds, introduce a change — cut to B-roll, change camera angle, add a graphic, pose a question — to reset viewer attention.

**Open loops:** Introduce a question or promise early in the video that you don't answer until later. "Stay with me, because at the end I'm going to show you [compelling thing]."

**Chapter markers / timestamps:** For YouTube, add timestamps in the description. Viewers who skip to relevant sections have higher completion rates than those who drop off because they can't find what they need.

**The "5 second rule" for pacing:** Every 5 seconds, a viewer should be receiving or anticipating value. If 5 seconds pass with no new information, entertainment, or anticipation — cut it.
