# Creative Concept Schema

Template for the `creative-concept.md` output. The creative concept is the single most important document the Creative Director produces — it governs every specialist brief and every review decision.

---

> **Developed by:** Creative Director
> **Date:** {{date}}
> **Status:** Approved / Draft

## The Idea

**Campaign idea:**
{{One sentence. The overarching creative platform — not a tagline, not a tactic. The idea behind all executions. Explains what the campaign is "about" at a level above any single execution.}}

*Example: "Show what it actually looks like when someone stops settling — using the real moments, real decisions, and real results of people who chose differently."*

**Campaign line:**
> "{{The headline, rallying phrase, or tagline that anchors all executions. Short, memorable, native to the brand voice.}}"

*Example: "No road. No rules."*

**Why this concept:**
{{2–3 sentences explaining the strategic logic. How does this idea address the audience psychology? How does it execute the core message? Why will it feel different from what competitors are saying?}}

## Creative Direction

**Visual direction:**
{{3–5 descriptive words or phrases that define the aesthetic world of this campaign. Specific enough for any designer to work from without a reference image. Can include: color mood, photography style, composition approach, typographic feel, production level.}}

*Examples:*
- *"Raw, high-contrast, shot-on-location — real people, no studio polish. Think editorial, not advertising."*
- *"Clean and minimal. White space does the work. Single bold color accent. Type-forward."*
- *"Warm, golden-hour light. Candid not posed. Product in context of use, never isolated."*

**Tone for this campaign:**
{{Which specific qualities within the brand voice this campaign leans into most. Reference voice-identity.md personality traits. Specify what is primary vs. secondary for this campaign moment.}}

*Example: "Lead with bold confidence — this campaign is assertive. The warmth is still there but plays second. We're not asking for the business, we're declaring something true."*

## What Makes a Strong Concept

Use this checklist when developing the concept before presenting to the user:

**The campaign idea:**
- [ ] Is one sentence — not a paragraph
- [ ] Describes the "what" of the campaign, not the tactic
- [ ] Could brief any specialist, in any channel, from this single sentence
- [ ] Is different from what competitors are doing (checked against ad landscape data)
- [ ] Connects to the audience psychology, not just the product features

**The campaign line:**
- [ ] Is 7 words or fewer (ideally 3–5)
- [ ] Feels native to the brand voice — would pass the NEVER list
- [ ] Is memorable on its own — doesn't require context to land
- [ ] Translates across channels — works as a Meta headline, email subject, social caption, billboard

**Visual direction:**
- [ ] A designer could start work from this description without further input
- [ ] Is consistent with the visual identity in voice-identity.md (colors, fonts, brand feel)
- [ ] Is specific — not "modern and clean" but what specific version of modern and clean
- [ ] Creates a distinct visual world — not generic to the category

**Tone calibration:**
- [ ] References specific traits from voice-identity.md, not generic descriptors
- [ ] Is a calibration, not a replacement — stays within the brand voice, shifts the emphasis

## Common Concept Failures to Avoid

**Too tactical:** "Run Meta video ads showing product features with a 20% off offer" — this is an execution plan, not a concept.

**Too generic:** "Show how our product helps real people" — every brand says this. What specific truth about real people, told in what specific way?

**Too product-focused:** A concept that leads with what the product does will always be weaker than a concept that leads with what the customer experiences. Products are the mechanism; the concept is the human truth.

**Campaign line is a sentence:** "Experience the difference that real quality makes in your everyday life" — too long, too generic, not memorable. A campaign line should feel like it could be on a billboard.

**Visual direction is a vibe board:** "Warm and inviting, with a touch of sophistication" — every brand in the category says this. Visual direction must be specific enough to exclude as well as include.
