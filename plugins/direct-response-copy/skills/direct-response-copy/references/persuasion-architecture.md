# Persuasion Architecture

Structural frameworks for conversion copy. Reference when building page outlines and writing specific sections.

---

## Awareness Levels (Schwartz)

The most important concept in direct response. Copy that does not match the reader's awareness level fails regardless of quality.

| Level | Reader State | Opening Strategy | Length |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know they have the problem | Open with a story or surprising fact that reveals the problem | Long — needs full context |
| **Problem Aware** | Knows the problem; doesn't know solutions exist | Open with empathy for the problem; agitate then introduce the solution category | Long-medium |
| **Solution Aware** | Knows solutions exist; comparing categories | Open with your approach's differentiator; prove superiority of method | Medium |
| **Product Aware** | Knows your product; not yet convinced | Open with the specific offer; overcome objections | Medium-short |
| **Most Aware** | Ready to buy; needs the trigger | Open with the offer and guarantee; create urgency | Short |

**Diagnosing awareness from traffic source:**
- Cold Meta/TikTok traffic: Problem Aware (most common)
- Cold Google Search (non-brand): Solution Aware
- Warm email list traffic: Product Aware
- Branded search / retargeting: Most Aware

## Page Section Jobs

Every section of a landing or sales page has exactly one job. Write to that job — not to the next section or the previous one.

| Section | Job | How to Know It's Working |
|---------|-----|------------------------|
| Headline | Stop the bounce; earn the next read | Would a stranger read past it? |
| Subheadline | Add specificity to the headline | Does it narrow and deepen the promise? |
| Hero CTA (above fold) | Catch the ready buyer | Does it ask for the conversion clearly? |
| Problem block | Create recognition and empathy | Does the reader feel seen? |
| Solution intro | Shift from problem to hope | Is there a clear pivot away from pain? |
| Product intro | Name and frame the offer | Is it clear what is being offered? |
| Features/benefits | Build desire | Does each feature translate to a felt benefit? |
| Proof block | Dissolve skepticism | Is the proof specific enough to be believed? |
| Objection block | Remove the last reasons not to buy | Are the real objections named and answered? |
| Guarantee | Transfer risk | Is the reader more protected after reading this? |
| CTA | Close | Is there one clear action? Does it feel low-risk? |

## Headline Formulas

The headline has one job: earn the read of the next line. Write 5–10 before choosing.

**Transformation formula:**
"[Timeframe], [From state] to [To state] — without [common obstacle]"
Example: "In 30 days, from inconsistent revenue to a predictable pipeline — without cold calling"

**Specific result formula:**
"How [audience] [got specific result] [in specific timeframe]"
Example: "How a bootstrapped SaaS founder hit $1M ARR without a sales team"

**Curiosity formula:**
"The [unexpected thing] that [unexpected outcome]"
Example: "The email that killed our biggest partnership — and tripled our sales"

**Direct offer formula:**
"Get [specific thing] [risk reversal]"
Example: "Get your first 100 subscribers in 30 days — guaranteed or we'll work with you until you do"

**Question formula (for Problem Aware audiences):**
"Are you [experiencing specific problem] — and wondering why [expected solution] isn't working?"

**Contrarian formula:**
"Why [common strategy everyone uses] is costing you [specific outcome]"

**Number formula:**
"[N] [audience type] use [thing/method] to [specific outcome]. Here's how."

## Proof Hierarchy

Not all proof is equal. Use the strongest available proof prominently; support with secondary proof types.

| Proof Type | Conversion Power | How to Use |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Specific testimonial with result | Very High | "Cut our CAC by 42% in Q1 — [Name, Title, Company]" |
| Video testimonial | Very High | Embed with transcript; human face = trust signal |
| Case study (detailed before/after) | High | Full story with problem, solution, result, metrics |
| Third-party review (G2, Trustpilot, etc.) | High | Star rating + review count + link to verify |
| Named customer logos | Medium-High | Use recognized brand names; logos in a row |
| Aggregate stats | Medium | "10,000+ customers" / "4.8/5 from 2,300 reviews" |
| Media mention / press | Medium | "As seen in [recognized publication]" |
| Credentials and expertise | Medium | Relevant credentials — not resume, but proof of standing |
| Mechanism explanation (logic proof) | Medium | "Here's the science behind why this works" |
| Awards | Lower | Use if from recognized body; little value otherwise |
| Generic testimonials ("I loved it!") | Very Low | Avoid; near-zero conversion impact |

**Testimonial structure for maximum impact:**
1. **Specific result** (leads): "Reduced our onboarding time from 14 days to 3"
2. **Context** (who they are): "We were a 40-person startup without a dedicated success team"
3. **Before state** (what was the problem): "We were losing customers in the first 30 days constantly"
4. **Attribution** (name, title, company): "Sarah K., VP of Customer Success, Acme SaaS"
5. **Photo** (if available — faces increase trust)

## Objection Handling Frameworks

### The Top 5 Universal Objections

| Objection | Subtext | Dissolve With |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| "Is this worth the price?" | I'm not sure the value justifies the cost | Value stack + anchor to alternatives + ROI calculation |
| "Will this work for me?" | My situation might be different | Social proof from people like them + specificity + guarantee |
| "I don't have time for this" | Implementation seems hard/slow | Emphasize speed of results + ease of use + time savings |
| "I've tried things like this before" | Been burned; skeptical | Name the alternatives and differentiate + stronger guarantee |
| "Not the right time" | Procrastination dressed as strategy | Cost of inaction + urgency + stakes of waiting |

### FAQ Format (most effective for objection handling)

FAQs are disarming — they feel like information, not selling. Format:

```
**Q: [The actual objection, phrased as the customer would ask it]**

A: [Direct, honest answer — address the real concern, then reframe toward the value. Do not dodge or be dismissive.]
```

**FAQ questions to always include if applicable:**
- "How long does it take to [get results / set up / learn the product]?"
- "What if I'm not [technical / a marketer / in that industry]?"
- "What if I don't like it?"
- "How is this different from [biggest competitor or most obvious alternative]?"
- "Is there a contract / can I cancel?"
- "Do I need [prerequisite that might feel like a barrier]?"

## Guarantee Writing Guide

The guarantee is an underutilized conversion lever. Strong guarantees close hesitant buyers.

### What makes a guarantee powerful:
1. **Specific terms** — "60-day" beats "satisfaction guaranteed"
2. **Specific outcome** — "If you don't [specific result] by [timeframe]..." beats "if you're not happy"
3. **Specific remedy** — "full refund" beats "we'll make it right"
4. **Confidence language** — write from a position of certainty in the product

### Guarantee formulas:

**Result-based:**
"If you don't [specific measurable outcome] within [timeframe], we'll refund every cent — no questions, no calls, no hoops."

**Try-it guarantee:**
"Use [product] for [N] days. If you don't love it, email us and we'll refund you in full. We'll even let you keep [product/bonus]."

**Double guarantee:**
"We guarantee [thing 1]. We also guarantee [thing 2]. If either fails, we'll [remedy]."

## CTA Architecture

The CTA section is not one line — it is a cluster of elements working together.

### CTA section anatomy:

```
[Brief re-statement of the transformation — 1 sentence]
[Price + what's included — the decision-maker's mental checklist]
[CTA button — specific action language]
[Microcopy — risk reversal or urgency in 1 line]
[Payment security note — "Secure checkout", card logos]
[Guarantee reminder — "60-day guarantee — if it's not for you, you pay nothing"]
```

### CTA button language:

Write button text that describes what happens after the click:
- "Start my free trial" > "Free trial"
- "Get instant access" > "Buy now"
- "Claim my [N]% discount" > "Get discount"
- "Yes — show me how it works" (conversational) — effective for skeptical audiences
- "I'm in — let's do this" (commitment language) — effective for motivated audiences

### Urgency — only honest urgency

Use urgency when it is true:
- Sale ends at a specific date and time: State the exact deadline
- Limited spots: State the actual number remaining
- Price increase: State when the price goes up and by how much

Do not use fake urgency (countdown timers that reset, false "limited spots" claims). Readers are increasingly sophisticated. Fake urgency backfires.

## VSL Script Structure

Video Sales Letters (VSLs) follow the same persuasion arc as sales pages, adapted for spoken delivery.

### 15-30 Minute VSL Structure

| Section | Timing | Copy Job |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Pattern interrupt hook | 0:00–0:10 | Stop the click-away in the first 10 seconds |
| Who this is for | 0:10–0:30 | Qualify the viewer — confirm they're in the right place |
| Big promise | 0:30–1:00 | State the transformation; create curiosity about how |
| Story / problem | 1:00–5:00 | Personal or customer story establishing the problem |
| Solution reveal | 5:00–8:00 | Introduce the product/method |
| Proof | 8:00–12:00 | Multiple testimonials and results |
| Mid-VSL CTA | 12:00 | Offer appears for the first time — for ready buyers |
| Objection handling | 12:00–18:00 | FAQ section; guarantee |
| Close | 18:00–20:00 | Final urgency; restated offer; strong CTA |
| Reminder for remaining viewers | 20:00+ | Brief; repeat the core promise and CTA |

**VSL writing rules:**
- Write for the ear: short sentences, conversational cadence, contractions ("you're" not "you are")
- Rhythm breaks: pause notes in the script signal natural speech stops
- No jargon without explanation: define anything the viewer might not know
- Call out the visual: note when something specific appears on screen
- Repeat the CTA: for VSLs over 15 minutes, show the offer at the midpoint and again at the end
